The Pre-Raphaelite Brotherhood (PRB) was a revolutionary group of young English painters, poets, and art critics founded in **1848**. The movement began in the visual arts but had a profound impact on Victorian poetry and design. It was a rebellion against the dull, academic art of their time, which they believed was based on a slavish imitation of the High Renaissance master, **Raphael**. They wanted to return to the art of the **early Italian Renaissance (before Raphael)**, which they admired for its vibrant color, detail, and sincere expression.

### Core Principles and Characteristics

*   **Truth to Nature**: This was their most important principle. They advocated for meticulous, almost photographic realism, studying nature attentively to represent it with truthfulness, simplicity, and a spirit of devotion.
*   **Vibrant Color and Sensuousness**: In rebellion against the drab colors of conventional Victorian painting, their works are startlingly bright and colorful. Their poetry is highly **sensuous** and **melodious**, appealing to the senses with vivid descriptions.
*   **Symbolism and Detail**: Despite their realism, their works are heavily symbolic. Every detailed object often carries a deeper moral or religious meaning.
*   **Medievalism and Literary Subjects**: They were fascinated by the medieval past, Arthurian legends, and the works of Dante, Shakespeare, and Keats. Their art often tells a literary story.
*   **Fusion of Flesh and Spirit**: The Pre-Raphaelites explored the connection between physical beauty and spiritual love, which was often controversial. This led to the hostile term **"The Fleshly School of Poetry,"** used by critic Robert Buchanan to attack their work as overly sensual and immoral.

<details class="hindi-translation">
<summary>🇮🇳 हिन्दी अनुवाद</summary>
*   **प्रकृति के प्रति सत्य (Truth to Nature)**: यह उनका सबसे महत्वपूर्ण सिद्धांत था। वे सूक्ष्म, लगभग फोटोग्राफिक यथार्थवाद की वकालत करते थे, प्रकृति का ध्यानपूर्वक अध्ययन करके इसे सच्चाई, सरलता, और भक्ति की भावना के साथ प्रस्तुत करने के लिए।
*   **जीवंत रंग और कामुकता (Vibrant Color and Sensuousness)**: पारंपरिक Victorian चित्रकला के फीके रंगों के विरुद्ध विद्रोह में, उनके काम आश्चर्यजनक रूप से चमकदार और रंगबिरंगे हैं। उनकी कविता अत्यधिक **कामुक (sensuous)** और **मधुर (melodious)** है, जीवंत वर्णनों के साथ इंद्रियों को आकर्षित करती है।
*   **प्रतीकवाद और विस्तार (Symbolism and Detail)**: अपनी यथार्थवादी प्रवृत्ति के बावजूद, उनके काम अत्यधिक प्रतीकात्मक हैं। हर विस्तृत वस्तु अक्सर एक गहरा नैतिक या धार्मिक अर्थ रखती है।
*   **मध्ययुगीनता और साहित्यिक विषय (Medievalism and Literary Subjects)**: वे मध्ययुगीन अतीत, Arthurian किंवदंतियों, और Dante, Shakespeare, और Keats के कामों से मोहित थे। उनकी कला अक्सर एक साहित्यिक कहानी कहती है।
*   **देह और आत्मा का मिलन (Fusion of Flesh and Spirit)**: Pre-Raphaelites ने शारीरिक सुंदरता और आध्यात्मिक प्रेम के बीच के संबंध का अन्वेषण किया, जो अक्सर विवादास्पद था। इससे आलोचक Robert Buchanan द्वारा उनके काम पर अत्यधिक कामुक और अनैतिक होने के लिए आक्रमण करने के लिए उपयोग किए गए विरोधी शब्द **"कामुक कविता का विद्यालय (The Fleshly School of Poetry)"** पैदा हुआ।

### Influences and Legacy

*   **Romantic Roots**: The Pre-Raphaelite movement is considered "a direct and legitimate development of the Romantic Revival in England." Their poetry has what one critic called "the smell of Coleridge's supernaturalism, Shelley's mysticism, Keats' sensuousness and Wordsworth's fancy of naturalism."
*   **Influence on Later Movements**: The Pre-Raphaelite Movement's emphasis on detailed symbolism and sensuousness was a major influence on the later **Aesthetic and Symbolist movements**.

<details class="hindi-translation">
<summary>🇮🇳 हिन्दी अनुवाद</summary>
*   **रोमांसिह मूल (Romantic Roots)**: Pre-Raphaelite आंदोलन को "इंग्लैंड में रोमांसिक पुनरुत्थान का एक प्रत्यक्ष और वैध विकास" माना जाता है। उनकी कविता में एक आलोचक के अनुसार "Coleridge के अलौकिकवाद (supernaturalism), Shelley के रहस्यवाद (mysticism), Keats की कामुकता (sensuousness) और Wordsworth के प्रकृतिवाद की कल्पना की खुशबू" है।
*   **बाद के आंदोलनों पर प्रभाव (Influence on Later Movements)**: Pre-Raphaelite आंदोलन का विस्तृत प्रतीकवाद और कामुकता पर जोर बाद के **सौंदर्यवादी और प्रतीकवादी आंदोलनों (Aesthetic and Symbolist movements)** पर प्रमुख प्रभाव था।
</details>

### Key Figures and Writings

The movement centered around a core group of founders and their associates. They also started a short-lived but influential journal called ***The Germ*** to promote their ideas.

<details class="hindi-translation">
<summary>🇮🇳 हिन्दी अनुवाद</summary>
यह आंदोलन संस्थापकों और उनके सहयोगियों के मुख्य समूह के चारों ओर केंद्रित था। उन्होंने अपने विचारों को बढ़ावा देने के लिए ***The Germ*** नामक एक अल्पकालिक लेकिन प्रभावशाली पत्रिका भी शुरू की।
</details>

*   **Dante Gabriel Rossetti (1828-1882)**: A painter and poet, he was the charismatic leader of the Brotherhood. His work is passionate, sensual, and mystical.
    *   *Key Writings:* ***"The Blessed Damozel,"*** *The House of Life* (sonnet sequence).
*   **Christina Rossetti (1830-1894)**: Sister of D.G. Rossetti and a major Victorian poet. Her poetry combines Pre-Raphaelite sensuousness with deep Anglican faith.
*   **William Morris (1834-1896)**: A poet, artist, designer, and socialist thinker. He championed craftsmanship in reaction against shoddy industrial goods.
*   **Algernon Charles Swinburne (1837-1909)**: A poet whose work, with its radical themes, musicality, and classical influences, is closely associated with the movement.
*   **John Everett Millais & William Holman Hunt**: The other two principal founders, primarily painters. Their works, like Millais's *Ophelia*, exemplify the PRB style.
*   **John Ruskin (1819-1900)**: Not a member, but the most powerful art critic of the era. He became the PRB's most important champion, praising their principle of "truth to nature" and urging artists to "go to nature."

**Summary for Quick Revision:**

The Pre-Raphaelite Brotherhood (PRB), founded in 1848, was a group of English painters, poets, and critics who rebelled against academic art, seeking to return to the detailed, vibrant, and sincere art of the early Italian Renaissance (before Raphael). Their core principles included "Truth to Nature" (meticulous realism), vibrant color, sensuousness, heavy symbolism, medievalism, and a fusion of flesh and spirit. Influenced by Romanticism, their work impacted later Aesthetic and Symbolist movements. Key figures include Dante Gabriel Rossetti (charismatic leader, poet), Christina Rossetti (poet), William Morris (poet, designer, socialist), Algernon Charles Swinburne (poet), and painters Millais and Hunt. Art critic John Ruskin was a key champion of the movement.
