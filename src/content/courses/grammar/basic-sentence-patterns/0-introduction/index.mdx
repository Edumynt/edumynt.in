# Welcome to Basic Sentence Patterns

If English sentences often feel difficult to control, the problem is usually not the number of rules. The problem is that the structure is still blurry.

This chapter sharpens that structure. You will keep meeting the same small set of sentence patterns until they start to feel familiar and usable.

## Who This Chapter Helps Most

This chapter is designed for learners who:

- know basic English vocabulary but hesitate while building sentences
- often translate from Hindi word by word
- want to understand *why* a sentence feels complete, incomplete, natural, or awkward
- need a practical base before moving to advanced grammar

## What Makes the Chapter Useful

1. **It starts from examples.** You see the pattern before you memorize the label.
2. **It respects Hindi interference.** The lessons directly address common transfer errors.
3. **It keeps returning to completeness.** A good sentence is not just grammatical in theory; it must also feel structurally finished.
4. **It asks you to judge, not just read.** The practice is there to build instinct.

## How to Use the Lessons

- Pause at every prompt and answer it yourself.
- If two patterns seem similar, compare the verb and ask what kind of completion it requires.
- Keep a small notebook of your own examples. Personal examples make structure stick.

When you are ready, begin with **What Makes a Sentence?** and focus first on one question: *what makes a group of words feel complete?*
