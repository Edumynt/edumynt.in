# Adding an Object — Subject + Verb + Object (SVO)

## Opening Hook

Read these two sentences:
- "She reads." — Complete, but...
- "She reads books." — Now we know WHAT she reads.

That little word — "books" — changes everything. It's called the **direct object**, and it's the key to the most common sentence pattern in English.

Today, we'll learn TWO things:
1. What a direct object is (and how to find it)
2. The #1 structural difference between Hindi and English

Let's start with the first.

---

## Part 1: The Direct Object

Look at these sentences:

| Sentence | Subject | Verb | What receives the action? |
|---|---|---|---|
| She reads books. | She | reads | books (what does she read?) |
| The dog chased the cat. | The dog | chased | the cat (what did it chase?) |
| I love music. | I | love | music (what do I love?) |

The **direct object** is the noun that receives the action. It answers "what?" or "whom?" after the verb.

**Verbs that need a direct object** are called **transitive verbs** (transitive = passing over). The action "passes over" from the subject to the object.

**Common transitive verbs:** eat, read, love, hit, buy, write, kick, open, close, drink, cook, watch, play.

> **Think about it:** "She eats" — is this complete? Grammatically yes, but it feels like something's missing. We want to know WHAT she eats. That's because "eat" is usually transitive.

---

## Practice: Find the Direct Object

1. "The chef cooked a delicious meal." → **a delicious meal** (cooked what?)
2. "Children love cartoons." → **cartoons** (love what?)
3. "She writes emails every day." → **emails** (writes what?)

---

## Part 2: The Big Word Order Shift

Now for the most important structural difference between Hindi and English.

**Hindi word order:** Subject + Object + Verb (SOV)
**English word order:** Subject + Verb + Object (SVO)

Let's see this in action:

| Hindi (SOV) | Literal Translation | Correct English (SVO) |
|---|---|---|
| मैं सेब खाता हूँ (Main seb khaata hoon) | "I apple eat" | "I eat an apple." |
| वह किताब पढ़ती है (Vah kitaab padhti hai) | "She book reads" | "She reads a book." |
| उसने गेंद को किक किया (Usne gend ko kick kiya) | "He ball kicked" | "He kicked the ball." |
| हम फिल्म देखते हैं (Hum film dekhte hain) | "We movie watch" | "We watch a movie." |

**See the pattern?** In Hindi, the verb comes LAST. In English, the verb comes in the MIDDLE.

This is the **#1 structural error** Hindi speakers make:

❌ "She book reads." ✅ "She reads a book."
❌ "I apple eat." ✅ "I eat an apple."

> **Think about it:** A Hindi speaker writes: "My friend to the market went." Fix it.

**Correct:** "My friend went to the market." (Subject + Verb + Place)

---

## Color-Coding the SVO Pattern

🔵 Subject + 🔴 Verb + 🟢 Object

```
🔵 She + 🔴 reads + 🟢 books
🔵 The dog + 🔴 chased + 🟢 the cat
🔵 I + 🔴 love + 🟢 music
```

When you write sentences, try to "see" this pattern. If your sentence doesn't follow 🔵🔴🟢, something might be wrong.

## Practice

**Identify S, V, and O:**
1. "The chef cooked a delicious meal." → S: The chef | V: cooked | O: a delicious meal
2. "Children love cartoons." → S: Children | V: love | O: cartoons

**Fix the word order:**
1. "I homework do." → "I do homework."
2. "She movie watched." → "She watched a movie."
3. "My mother food cooks." → "My mother cooks food."

**Translate from Hindi:**
1. "मैं रोज व्यायाम करता हूँ" → "I exercise every day."
2. "उसने एक पत्र लिखा" → "He/She wrote a letter."
3. "वह गाना गाती है" → "She sings a song."

## Key Takeaways
1. The **direct object** receives the action (answers "what?" or "whom?").
2. **Transitive verbs** need a direct object.
3. Hindi is **SOV**. English is **SVO**. Verb goes in the MIDDLE.
4. Color pattern: 🔵 Subject + 🔴 Verb + 🟢 Object
