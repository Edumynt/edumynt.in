# Basic Sentence Patterns

This chapter gives you the structural backbone of English sentences.

Instead of treating grammar as hundreds of disconnected rules, we start with a smaller question: *what basic shape is this sentence using?* Once you can answer that, many grammar problems become easier to diagnose.

For Hindi-speaking learners, this chapter is especially useful because many mistakes are not vocabulary mistakes. They happen when the sentence is built in a Hindi-like order or when a verb is given the wrong kind of completion.

## What You'll Learn

| Lesson | Pattern | Main focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What is a sentence? | Sentence vs fragment, subject, predicate, and clause completeness |
| 2 | SV | Verbs that stand complete without an object |
| 3 | SVO | Direct objects and the Hindi SOV to English SVO shift |
| 4 | SVOO | Verbs that involve a receiver and a thing transferred |
| 5 | SVC | Linking verbs and complements that describe the subject |
| 6 | SVOC | Object complements and result/descriptive patterns |
| 7 | SVA & SVOA | Optional vs required adverbials |
| 8 | Pattern workshop | Mixed analysis, correction, and sentence building |

## How to Study This Chapter Well

1. Read the examples slowly and say them aloud.
2. Try each prompt before checking the explanation.
3. Notice not only the correct answer, but *why nearby wrong answers feel tempting*.
4. When possible, create one sentence of your own after each lesson.

## What Matters Most Here

By the end of this chapter, you should not only remember labels such as SV or SVO. You should be able to look at a sentence and explain why the verb stands alone, needs an object, needs a complement, or needs a place/time expression to feel complete.
