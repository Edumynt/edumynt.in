# The Simplest Pattern — Subject + Verb (SV)

## Opening Hook

Here are some sentences. What do they have in common?

- "Birds fly."
- "The baby cried."
- "Time flies."
- "She laughed."

They're all short. They're all complete. And they all follow the SAME pattern.

Let's figure out what that pattern is.

---

## Discovering the SV Pattern

Look at the sentences again:

| Sentence | Who/What? | What happens? |
|---|---|---|
| Birds fly. | Birds | fly |
| The baby cried. | The baby | cried |
| She laughed. | She | laughed |
| The sun rises. | The sun | rises |

Every sentence has TWO parts: **who/what** (subject) + **what happens** (verb). That's it.

This is the **SV pattern**: 🔵 Subject + 🔴 Verb

```
🔵 Subject → 🔴 Verb
```

Some verbs don't need anything else. "Fly" — fly what? It doesn't matter. Birds just fly. The action is complete by itself.

> **Think about it:** Can you add an object to "The baby sleeps"? "The baby sleeps a blanket"? No — "sleep" doesn't need an object. It's complete by itself.

---

## Intransitive Verbs (Verbs That Stand Alone)

Verbs that don't need an object are called **intransitive verbs** (in = not, transitive = passing over). The action doesn't "pass over" to an object — it stays with the subject.

**Common intransitive verbs:**

| Verb | Example |
|---|---|
| sleep | "The baby sleeps." |
| arrive | "The train arrived." |
| laugh | "She laughed." |
| cry | "He cried." |
| run | "They run every morning." |
| fall | "Leaves fall in autumn." |
| wait | "I'm waiting." |

> **Think about it:** Is "die" transitive or intransitive? Can you "die something"?

No — "die" is intransitive. "The plant died." Complete sentence.

---

## Some Verbs Can Be BOTH!

Here's where it gets interesting:

| Intransitive (no object) | Transitive (with object) |
|---|---|
| "She runs every morning." | "She runs a company." |
| "He reads before bed." | "He reads books." |
| "The door opened." | "She opened the door." |

**How do you know which it is?** Look at what comes after the verb. If there's a direct object (something receiving the action), it's transitive. If not, it's intransitive.

---

## Hindi Word Order

Good news: for intransitive verbs, Hindi and English word order is the **same**!

- Hindi: "बच्चा रोया" (Bachcha roya) — Baby cried
- English: "The baby cried."

But watch out: ❌ "Cried the baby." ✅ "The baby cried." Subject BEFORE verb, always.

---

## Adding Information (Adverbials)

"Birds fly" is complete, but we can add information:

| Core SV | With Added Information |
|---|---|
| Birds fly. | Birds fly **south in winter**. |
| The baby cried. | The baby cried **loudly**. |
| She laughed. | She laughed **at the joke**. |

These added words are called **adverbials** — they tell us more about the action (where, when, how). We'll learn much more about adverbials in Lesson 7. For now, just know: adding an adverbial doesn't change the pattern. "Birds fly south" is still SV.

> **Think about it:** "She slept peacefully" — is "peacefully" an object?

**No!** It's an adverbial. It tells us HOW she slept. "Sleep" is still intransitive.

---

## Practice

**Identify the SV pattern (no direct object!):**
1. "The flowers bloomed." → **SV** ✅
2. "She reads books." → **Not SV** (has object — SVO)
3. "The train arrived late." → **SV** ✅ ("late" is adverbial, not object)

**Transitive or intransitive?**
- "She runs every morning." → **Intransitive**
- "She runs a business." → **Transitive**

## 🏅 Badge Earned: "Pattern Finder"

You can now identify the SV pattern and distinguish transitive from intransitive verbs!

---

## Key Takeaways
1. SV = Subject + Verb (simplest pattern).
2. **Intransitive verbs** don't need objects.
3. Some verbs can be both transitive and intransitive.
4. **Adverbials** add information but don't change the pattern. (More in Lesson 7!)
