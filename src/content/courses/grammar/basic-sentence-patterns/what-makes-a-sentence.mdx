# What Makes a Sentence?

## Opening Hook

Look at these three groups of words:

**Group A:**
- "The cat sat on the mat."
- "She reads books every day."
- "What a beautiful morning!"

**Group B:**
- "On the mat."
- "Every day in the morning."
- "The tall girl with."

> **Think about it:** What's the difference between Group A and Group B? Which group feels "complete"? Write your answer before reading on.

Group A feels complete. Each one tells you something — a fact, an action, or an exclamation. Group B leaves you hanging. You want to know: "On the mat — WHAT happened on the mat?" or "The tall girl with — WHAT about her?"

That's the difference between a **sentence** and a **fragment**. And that's what today is about.

---

## What Is a Sentence?

A sentence is a group of words that expresses a **complete thought**. It needs two things:

1. **A subject** — the person or thing the sentence is about (the "who" or "what")
2. **A predicate** — what the subject does or what happens to it (the "action" or "state")

Let's see this in action:

| Sentence | Subject (Who/What?) | Predicate (What happens?) |
|---|---|---|
| Birds fly. | Birds | fly |
| The sun rises in the east. | The sun | rises in the east |
| She is a doctor. | She | is a doctor |

> **Think about it:** "The dog barked loudly at the stranger." What's the subject? What's the predicate?

Subject: **The dog** (the sentence is about the dog)
Predicate: **barked loudly at the stranger** (that's what the dog did)

## The Four Types of Sentences

Sentences do different jobs. Let's meet all four:

### 1. Declarative Sentences (Statements)
These tell you something. They end with a period (.).
- "The Earth revolves around the Sun."
- "I enjoy reading novels."

> **Think about it:** Translate this Hindi statement into English: "मुझे चाय पसंद है" (Mujhe chai pasand hai). Don't translate word-for-word!

**"I like tea."** (Not: "To me tea is liked." — that's Hindi word order!)

### 2. Interrogative Sentences (Questions)
These ask something. They end with a question mark (?).
- "Where do you live?"
- "Is she coming to the party?"

### 3. Imperative Sentences (Commands/Requests)
These tell someone to do something. The subject "you" is implied.
- "Please sit down."
- "Stop!" (means "YOU stop!")

### 4. Exclamatory Sentences (Exclamations)
These express strong emotion. They end with an exclamation mark (!).
- "What a beautiful painting!"
- "How fast she runs!"

## Sentence vs. Fragment: The Completeness Test

| Fragment (Incomplete) | Why | Sentence (Complete) |
|---|---|---|
| "Running down the street." | Who is running? | "The boy is running down the street." |
| "Because I was tired." | Tells WHY, not WHAT. | "I went to bed early because I was tired." |
| "The tall girl with the red bag." | What about her? | "The tall girl with the red bag is my sister." |

> **Think about it:** Is this a sentence or a fragment? "After the rain stopped."

**Fragment.** It tells WHEN but not WHAT. Fix: "After the rain stopped, we went outside."

## Practice

**Identify the sentence type (D=Declarative, I=Interrogative, Im=Imperative, E=Exclamatory):**
1. "Please pass me the salt." → **Im**
2. "The train arrives at 6 PM." → **D**
3. "Did you see the news?" → **I**
4. "What an incredible performance!" → **E**

**Sentence or fragment?**
1. "The children played in the park." → **Sentence**
2. "In the park near the fountain." → **Fragment**
3. "She sings beautifully." → **Sentence**

## Key Takeaways
1. A sentence = subject + predicate = complete thought.
2. Four types: Declarative, Interrogative, Imperative, Exclamatory.
3. A fragment is an incomplete thought.
4. The simplest sentence needs just a subject and a verb.
