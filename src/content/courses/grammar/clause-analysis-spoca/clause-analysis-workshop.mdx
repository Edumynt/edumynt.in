# Clause Analysis Workshop

This is your final detective round.

You now know the five clause elements. The goal is to look at a sentence and answer one question clearly:

**What job is each part doing?**

---

## Round 1: Basic Analysis

### 1. "The girls laughed loudly."
- S = **The girls**
- P = **laughed**
- A = **loudly**

### 2. "My brother bought a bicycle."
- S = **My brother**
- P = **bought**
- O = **a bicycle**

### 3. "The tea smells wonderful."
- S = **The tea**
- P = **smells**
- C = **wonderful**

### 4. "They sent me an invitation."
- S = **They**
- P = **sent**
- O = **me**
- O = **an invitation**

### 5. "She kept the milk in the fridge."
- S = **She**
- P = **kept**
- O = **the milk**
- A = **in the fridge**

---

## Round 2: Spot the Mistake

### Example A
**Wrong:** "She put the vase."  
**Problem:** required adverbial missing  
**Better:** "She put the vase on the shelf."

### Example B
**Wrong:** "The boy in the room happy."  
**Problem:** the predicator is missing  
**Better:** "The boy in the room is happy."

### Example C
**Wrong:** "They made him."  
**Problem:** expected completion missing in many contexts  
**Better:** "They made him captain." / "They made him happy."

## Round 3: Full SPOCA Thinking

Analyze these carefully:

1. "The judge found the witness honest."  
   - S = **The judge**  
   - P = **found**  
   - O = **the witness**  
   - C = **honest**

2. "Our guests stayed at a hotel."  
   - S = **Our guests**  
   - P = **stayed**  
   - A = **at a hotel**

3. "The story gave the children hope."  
   - S = **The story**  
   - P = **gave**  
   - O = **the children**  
   - O = **hope**

4. "The sky turned orange."  
   - S = **The sky**  
   - P = **turned**  
   - C = **orange**

---

## Self-Check Method

Whenever you feel confused, use this checklist:

1. Find the verb group.
2. Find who or what the clause is about.
3. Ask: does the verb act on something?
4. Ask: is anything renaming or describing subject/object?
5. Ask: is any place/time/manner element present or required?

If you can answer those five questions, you can usually analyze the clause correctly.

## Final Challenge

Try these on your own before checking mentally:

- "The child seems nervous."
- "We offered the visitor tea."
- "Rohan placed his bag near the door."
- "The class elected Meera leader."
- "The train arrived late."

Suggested analyses:

- The child seems nervous. → S + P + C
- We offered the visitor tea. → S + P + O + O
- Rohan placed his bag near the door. → S + P + O + A
- The class elected Meera leader. → S + P + O + C
- The train arrived late. → S + P + A

## One More Independent Round

Analyze these before looking at the hints:

- "The manager gave the staff new instructions."
- "The sky looks clear."
- "She placed her notebook beside the lamp."

Hints:

- one has two objects
- one has a subject complement
- one has an object plus required adverbial

## Key Takeaways

1. SPOCA turns grammar into a visible structure.
2. Errors often happen when one required clause element is missing or misidentified.
3. Clause analysis improves writing, translation, and exam performance.
4. Once you see clause jobs clearly, English becomes easier to control.
