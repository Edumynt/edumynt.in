# Clause Analysis in Terms of SPOCA

If sentence patterns show you the big architecture of English, **SPOCA** gives you the inspection tool.

> **Bridge note:** In the previous chapter, you learned patterns such as **SV, SVO, SVC, SVOO, SVOC, SVA, and SVOA**. In this chapter, we use **P = Predicator** instead of just **V = Verb**, because we want to focus on the **whole verb group**. So earlier **SV** becomes **SP**, **SVO** becomes **SPO**, and so on.

SPOCA stands for **Subject, Predicator, Object, Complement, Adverbial**. With it, you can look inside a clause and ask:

- Who or what is the clause about?
- What is happening?
- Is something receiving the action?
- Is something being renamed or described?
- Is any place, time, manner, or direction required?

This chapter is especially useful for Hindi-speaking learners because many English errors happen when we know the meaning but cannot see the **job** each part is doing inside the clause.

## Why This Chapter Matters

Many students can translate words but still produce broken English like:

- "She put the book."  
- "He made me."  
- "The boy in the room happy."

The problem is not vocabulary. The problem is **incomplete clause analysis**. The verb is asking for something, and the clause has not supplied it correctly.

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to look at a sentence like a grammar detective and label every important part with confidence.
