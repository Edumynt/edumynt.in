# Clause Analysis in Terms of SPOCA

If sentence patterns give you the outer shape of a sentence, **SPOCA** helps you look inside that shape and explain what each part is doing.

In the previous chapter you worked with labels such as **SV, SVO, SVC, SVOO, SVOC, SVA, and SVOA**. In this chapter, the focus shifts from pattern names to clause functions:

- **S** = Subject
- **P** = Predicator
- **O** = Object
- **C** = Complement
- **A** = Adverbial

That shift matters because many grammar problems are really problems of clause analysis. The learner knows the intended meaning, but cannot yet see what the verb requires or how later elements function.

## Why This Chapter Matters

Many learners understand the words in a sentence but still produce incomplete or awkward clauses such as:

- "She put the book."
- "He made me."
- "The boy in the room happy."

These are not mainly vocabulary problems. They are structure problems.

This chapter trains you to ask sharper questions:

- What is the full verb group?
- What is the clause about?
- Is there an object?
- Is a complement needed?
- Is the location/time phrase optional, or is the verb leaning on it?

## How to Use the Chapter

Move slowly. SPOCA is most useful when you pause at each example and try the analysis yourself before reading the explanation. If two labels seem possible, compare them and ask what test settles the issue.
