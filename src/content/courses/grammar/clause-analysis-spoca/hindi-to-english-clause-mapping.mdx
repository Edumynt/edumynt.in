# Hindi to English Clause Mapping

Many learners do not make errors because they do not know words. They make errors because they carry Hindi meaning into English without rebuilding the clause.

SPOCA gives you a repair method.

---

## Step 1: Identify the Main Process

Ask: **What is happening?**

Example Hindi idea:

**"उसने किताब मेज़ पर रखी।"**

Main process = **put/placed**

Once you choose the English verb, the clause pattern begins to reveal itself.

- Subject = she
- Predicator = put
- Object = the book
- Adverbial = on the table

Final sentence: **"She put the book on the table."**

---

## Step 2: Ask What the Verb Requires

Compare:

### Example A
**"वह सोया।"**
- S = he
- P = slept
- Full clause: **He slept.**

### Example B
**"वह दिल्ली में रहता है।"**
- S = he
- P = lives
- A = in Delhi
- Full clause: **He lives in Delhi.**

English **live** strongly expects a location in many beginner-level uses.

---

## Step 3: Distinguish Object from Complement

Hindi idea:

**"वे उसे कप्तान चुनते हैं।"**

Wrong instinct: treat both following parts as ordinary objects.

Better analysis:
- S = they
- P = elected / choose
- O = him
- C = captain

Final sentence: **"They elected him captain."**

---

## Step 4: Watch Word-for-Word Traps

### Trap 1
Hindi: **"मुझे चाय पसंद है।"**  
Wrong: "To me tea is liked."

Correct clause analysis:
- S = I
- P = like
- O = tea

Final sentence: **"I like tea."**

### Trap 2
Hindi: **"कमरा ठंडा हो गया।"**
- S = the room
- P = became
- C = cold

Final sentence: **"The room became cold."**

### Trap 3
Hindi: **"उसने मुझे एक सवाल पूछा।"**
- S = she
- P = asked
- O = me
- O = a question

Final sentence: **"She asked me a question."**

---

## Practical Strategy

When translating from Hindi to English, do this in order:

1. Choose the main English verb.
2. Mark the **Subject**.
3. Mark the **Predicator**.
4. Ask whether the verb needs an **Object**.
5. Ask whether any element is a **Complement**.
6. Ask whether a required **Adverbial** is missing.
7. Only then build the final sentence.

This prevents blind word-for-word translation.

## Common Hindi-to-English Clause Traps

Here are some frequent problems SPOCA helps you catch:

- **missing subject** → "Is raining" instead of "It is raining."
- **missing predicator part** → "He going" instead of "He is going."
- **missing object** → "She bought" when the intended meaning needs *what?*
- **object/complement confusion** → "They made him" instead of "They made him captain."
- **missing required adverbial** → "She put the bag" instead of "She put the bag on the chair."

## Guided Practice

Analyze before translating:

1. **"बच्चे मैदान में खेल रहे हैं।"**  
   - S = **The children**  
   - P = **are playing**  
   - A = **in the field**

2. **"वह बहुत खुश लग रही है।"**  
   - S = **She**  
   - P = **looks / seems**  
   - C = **very happy**

3. **"उन्होंने दीवार नीली रंग दी।"**  
   - S = **They**  
   - P = **painted**  
   - O = **the wall**  
   - C = **blue**

> **Think about it:** Before translating a Hindi sentence, which is more important first: the words or the clause pattern?

The safer answer is: **the clause pattern**.

## Try These Before Checking

Translate by doing SPOCA analysis first:

1. **"उसने बच्चे को कहानी सुनाई।"**
2. **"कमरा शांत हो गया।"**
3. **"उन्होंने किताब मेज़ पर रख दी।"**

### Suggested Answers

1. **She told the child a story.** → S + P + O + O
2. **The room became quiet.** → S + P + C
3. **They put the book on the table.** → S + P + O + A

## Key Takeaways

1. Translation becomes easier when you map meaning onto SPOCA.
2. English verbs control clause shape in important ways.
3. Word-for-word translation fails when clause roles are ignored.
